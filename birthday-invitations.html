What to put on a birthday invitation

Guests look for three things first: whose birthday it is, when and where. Put the name in the title, such as "Mia turns seven", so the card answers the first question at a glance.

  • Who and which birthday. Name a milestone age. For an adult who would rather not, "Join us to celebrate Sam" works.
  • Date, start time and end time. For children, an end time tells parents when to come back.
  • Venue and address. The invite link adds a map button for the address you type.
  • What to expect. A line about food, games, a theme or a dress code saves you a dozen questions.
  • How to reply and by when. A reply date a week before the party gives you time to order cake.

Wording ideas

You're invited to Mia's seventh birthday. Cake, games and a piñata. Bring your best party hat.

Leo is turning one! Come celebrate his first trip around the sun with us.

Forty and fabulous. Join us for dinner, drinks and dancing in honor of Dana.

Shh, it's a surprise! Arrive by 6:30 so we can all yell together.

For a surprise party, say so near the top, give an arrival time earlier than the guest of honor, and never send the link to a shared family calendar. The invitation wording guide has more examples for every line of the card.

When to send it

Send birthday invitations two to three weeks ahead. Children's parties fill weekends fast, so three weeks is safer during the school year. For a milestone birthday with travel, give guests six weeks. When to send invitations covers timing for every occasion.
