Invitation wording guide

What to write on each line of an invitation, from the first words to the reply date.

A good invitation answers who, what, when and where before a guest has to scroll. Every Petalpost template has the same lines in the same order, so this guide follows that order.

The opening line

The small line above the title sets the tone. "You're invited to" works for anything. "Please join us for" is a little warmer, and "Together with their families" opens a formal wedding invitation. Leave it out if the title already says it all.

The title

The title is what guests remember, so make it the event, not the template. "Mia turns seven" beats "Birthday party". "Lena and Omar" is enough for a wedding. For a shower or a housewarming, name the people it is for: "Baby shower for Priya", "Sam and Jo's new place".

The hosts

Say who is inviting. Guests reply to the host, and for a shower or a surprise party the host is not the guest of honor. Examples: "Hosted by Ana and the Lopez family", "Join Mia and her family", "With love from the bridesmaids".

Date and time

Give the day of the week as well as the date, since that is how most people check their calendar. Give an end time when it matters: children's parties, open houses and anything with a booking. Petalpost writes the date out in full and adds a calendar button to the invite link, so guests do not have to copy it by hand.

  • Formal: "Saturday, the twelfth of June, at four o'clock in the afternoon".
  • Everyday: "Saturday, June 12, 4:00 to 7:00 PM".
  • Open house: "Drop by any time between 2 and 6".

Venue and address

Put the venue name and the full street address on separate lines. Guests search for the name and navigate to the address. Add anything a first-time visitor needs: the floor, a buzzer code, where to park, or "around the back, through the garden gate".

The note

One or two sentences about what to expect. This is the place for food, a theme, a dress code, gifts, or who is welcome. Keep it short; the card has room for a line or two, not a paragraph.

Cake, games and a piñata. Bring your best party hat.

Dinner and dancing to follow. Cocktail attire.

No gifts, just you. Little ones very welcome.

Mulled cider, board games and a very friendly cat.

The reply line

Ask for a reply by a specific date, not "as soon as possible", and say who to reply to. "RSVP to Ana by December 5" is clear. If you publish the invitation on Petalpost, guests reply from the link and you see every answer in one place.

Formal or casual?

Match the wording to the event, then keep it consistent from the first line to the last.

  • Formal: full names, numbers written as words, "request the pleasure of your company", and no exclamation marks. Suits weddings, milestone anniversaries and black-tie dinners.
  • Casual: first names, numerals, contractions and a joke if it fits. Suits birthdays, brunches and housewarmings.

Mistakes guests notice

  • A date and a weekday that do not match. Check the calendar twice.
  • No reply date, so half the replies arrive the day before.
  • An address without a city or a unit number.
  • Plus-ones or children left unclear, so guests have to ask.
  • A note so long that the key details get lost.
