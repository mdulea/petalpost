What a wedding invitation says

Traditional wording follows a fixed order that guests expect:

  • The hosts. Whoever is hosting invites the guests: the couple, their parents, or both families together.
  • The request line. "Request the pleasure of your company" is the classic phrase. "Invite you to celebrate" is lighter.
  • The couple. First names, or full names when the families host.
  • Date and time. Formal cards spell them out; a casual card can use numerals.
  • Ceremony venue and address. If the reception is somewhere else, say so in the note.
  • Reply details. Who to reply to and by when. Ask about dietary needs on the reply, not on the card.

Wording ideas

Together with their families, Lena and Omar request the pleasure of your company as they marry.

Join us as we say I do, then stay for dinner and dancing under the stars.

We eloped! Now let us celebrate with you. Garden party to follow.

Keep the note short. Dress code, children and plus-ones belong on the invite only when they change who comes or what they wear. Longer details, such as hotels and travel, fit better on a wedding website you link to from the note.

When to send it

Send save-the-dates six to eight months ahead, or up to a year for a destination wedding. Send the invitation itself six to eight weeks before the day, with a reply date three to four weeks before it so you can give the caterer final numbers. See when to send invitations for the full timeline.
